### Round 3 — Phase 6 only — 2026-08-11 — same clean-room agent, resumed — **READY**

`VERDICT: READY`, zero blocking. Round 2's blocker was resolved **by removal
rather than by patching**: `nodeDots` stopped grouping. The reviewer re-ran the
fan that broke the clustering rule under all six permutations of `doc.links` and
got the same three positions every time, against the measured 2/1/2/2/1/2 before.

Three non-blocking findings folded in, and each changed the document:

- **A measurement quoted as a bound was an instance.** The `3.55e-15` float slack
  I measured is not the worst case — a 400-split scan reached **2.84e-14**, and
  the slack grows with distance from the world origin (≈1.6e-10 at 1e6 units).
  The margin argument survives untouched (breakeven needs a drawing 10¹⁰ units
  out) but §2.10.2 now says worst-observed rather than implying a bound, and adds
  the other side of the margin the round measured: the nearest **genuinely
  distinct** pair of lateral shifts the UI can produce is `0.45` apart, from 37
  enumerated values.
- **The one assertion the epsilon answers to had a fixture that would not
  discriminate.** 314 of those 400 splits are bitwise identical, and the obvious
  reading of "an uneven split" — a third of a 45° diagonal — measures exactly `0`,
  so an exact-equality dedupe passes it and the epsilon goes untested. The gate
  now carries a measured recipe (`(0,0)`→`(97,233)`, waypoint at `f = 0.95`) and,
  more usefully, the property any substitute must have.
- **The six-permutation assertion would fail a correct implementation.**
  `nodeDots` returns its positions in `junctionArms`' order, so permuting the
  links legitimately permutes the array; the gate now says compare **as a set**.

Two smaller ones taken: `[]` for a node with no layout entry, and the aligned
undivided endpoint, both gained assertions of their own. And the round noticed
`geometry.ts:SAME_EDGE` is already `1e-6` and already documented as this kind of
guard — taken as evidence the magnitude is precedented, but **not** reused: the
two answer different questions, and a shared constant is how tuning one silently
retunes the other (`zk-012`'s `geometry_length` lesson).

One wording nit accepted: "order-independent by construction" is by construction
for the bitwise-equal pairs and by quantisation for the rest, and now says so.

### Round 2 — Phase 6 only — 2026-08-11 — same clean-room agent, resumed — **NOT READY**

One new blocking finding, and it killed the design rather than adjusting it.

**Greedy grouping by side was unbound and order-dependent.** `dot(v_a, v_b) > 0`
never said what `v_b` was — a group has several displacements, and comparing
against its first member, its running mean or all its members are three different
rules. Worse, "same side" is not transitive. Measured on a document built
entirely through the reducer — three `offside`-aligned links off one endpoint node
at `0°`, `60°`, `109.9°` — the three readings give 2, 1 and 2 dots, and holding
the first-member reading fixed while permuting `doc.links` gives **2, 1, 2, 2, 1,
2**. So the *number of dots drawn* depended on the order the human happened to
draw the links in, and nothing in the gate's five rows had a group large enough
to see it.

**Adjudication: accepted, and the fix deletes the mechanism.** Binding `v_b` only
chooses which of the six readings you get. §2.10.2 was rewritten around **one dot
per distinct arm origin** — no angle, no dot product, no mean, no group, no
ordering — so order-independence stops being an argument and becomes a property
of returning a set. The cost is taken deliberately and stated: a lane-drop divided
waypoint now draws **four** dots rather than two, because those genuinely are two
road ends at two places. That is round 1's blocker answered by taking its *other*
branch, not by tuning a tolerance.

Both "recorded limits" went with the rule they qualified — the right-angle
boundary and the three-arm order-dependence are not limits of a rule with no
angles in it. Three non-blocking findings also folded: the right-angle limit was
off by its boundary anyway (exactly `0` at 90°, measured independently); a
zero-length first segment puts a *divided* arm's origin back on the node, via
`offsetPolyline`'s unguarded endpoint branches, which under the new rule needs no
case at all because the dot follows the drawn road end wherever it is; and a
sentence about the taper wedge described a row that draws no wedge.

Clean-room reviewer with repo access. It **confirmed §2.2's load-bearing

### Round 2 — 2026-07-25 — `VERDICT: NOT READY` (1 blocking, newly introduced)

Same reviewer, resumed. It confirmed all eight round-1 blockers resolved, and
checked three of the new mechanisms numerically: the reach floor never binds at
the default Size so the existing pad test (`Diagram.test.tsx:213`) still passes;
`ringT`/`ri` derive from `ro` (`:413-414`) so they inherit the floor for free;

The one new blocker was **introduced by round 1's own §2.4 rewrite**. Round 1's
rule gated on "the two drawn lane regions differ in width", which excluded a bend
by construction; replacing it with edge *coincidence* on world points dropped
that guard. `segmentNormals` (`geometry.ts:316-325`) rotates with the link, so at
`N1(0,0) → N2(120,0) → N3(120,120)` two **identical** 4-lane links put their
nearside casing edges at `(120, 19.5)` and `(100.5, 0)` — a plain corner would
have drawn two wedges and two butt caps, silently contradicting Phase 3's own
"markup unchanged" gate, which a collinear fixture would never catch. Fixed by
making the comparison one of **signed lateral offsets in the shared frame**
(`d ± roadWidth/2`, which `drawnPolyline` already computes) and adding a
`TAPER_MAX_BEND` collinearity guard, with the corner as an explicit Phase 3 gate
case.

Non-blocking, folded in: Phase 4 now names `persist.rs`'s
`rejects_a_newer_schema_version` fixture (`:120-137`), which writes
`schema_version: 2` and must go to `3` or silently stop testing anything; §2.2
records that Size **clamps** below ~half scale (an intended consequence of the
unscaled floor, not a bug) and Phase 1's pin is explicitly written at the default
Size; `dist` → `distance` (`geometry.ts:57`).

### Round 3 — 2026-07-25 — `VERDICT: READY` (0 blocking) — **converged**

Same reviewer, resumed. It re-derived the new rule rather than reading it:
confirmed `segmentNormals` produces the spec's `(120, 19.5)` / `(100.5, 0)`
counter-example exactly; reproduced **all four rows** of §2.4's table from
`d ± roadWidth/2` (drop → inset downstream, addition → inset upstream,
`centre` → both sides, mismatched alignment → one wedge each way); and confirmed
that because offsets are frame-independent scalars, equality holds at *any* bend
within tolerance, so a bend with no width step keeps its present-day markup.

Three non-blocking refinements folded in at convergence:

- **A rationale sentence was mis-attributed** (a real correction, not style):
  §2.4 credited the reversed-twin exclusion with making the two frames agree in
  sign. It does not — a hairpin `N1→N2`, `N2→N3` with N3 beside N1 has a
  different node pair, so the twin test misses it, and its frames oppose. The
  **bend guard** is what makes the claim true. Rewritten so a later reader cannot
  drop the guard believing the twin test covers it.
- **Butt caps notch the outside of a bend** — depth `(roadWidth/2)·tan(θ/2)`,
  which the round caps used to fill. This turned `TAPER_MAX_BEND` from a picked
  number into a derived one: **8°, not 15°**, so that at the tolerance limit the
  notch (≈1.36 units, 4-lane) is no deeper than the ≈1.33-unit overhang the butt
  cap removes. 15° would have inverted the trade at ≈2.6. Added as the third
  consequence in §2.4's list.
- Phase 3's gate said "equal casing edges"; now "equal casing-edge **offsets**",
  matching §2.4's language.

**Converged at three rounds.** `status: draft` → `reviewed`; the phases are
cleared for plan-mode implementation.
