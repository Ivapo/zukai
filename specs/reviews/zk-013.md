### Phase 1 — SHIPPED 2026-08-10 — as built

No deviation from the reviewed design. 423 vitest (up 17), `cargo test` unchanged
at 68, no Rust, no model change, no `SCHEMA_VERSION` move. **OQ-5 taken by the
user** — floor the badge rather than let it vanish — with one refinement made at
plan time and recorded in the question itself: the floor is **conditional on `s`
being 0**, because an unconditional one raises §2.3's own 1-lane-through/4-lane-stem
T from a measured 6 to 9.51 and restores the yellow the measured bound removes.
**OQ-3 and OQ-6 were both answered by drawing them**, which is what they asked for.

What the round-4 record predicted held, and two things it did not name came out of
the build:

- **The gate's own claim about itself was verifiable, and was verified.** Six
  mutations, each failing a distinct test; the stop bars, hit disc and halo were
  byte-identical under **all six**, which is §2.3's "regression check, not a proof"
  demonstrated rather than asserted.
- **The winding discipline needed an assertion the spec did not ask for.** §2.2
  states it as a construction rule, and the construction is safe — the arm's frame
  is a rotation — but a reversed ring cancels into a hole *where the roads meet*,
  and the gate's `inside(rings, p)` calls that point covered because it asks one
  ring at a time. Pinned on the signed area.
- **The Size floor makes the pad round again**, found in the dev pass: at
  `r = width / 2` a band's intersection with the disc **is** a half-disc. The clamp
  working, not the shape failing — but it is the one thing a reader might report as
  a bug, so it is recorded in three places.

### Round 4 — document-wide — 2026-08-10 — both round-1 agents, resumed — **READY** (converged)

**Both reviewers `VERDICT: READY`, zero blocking.** Run past the cap on the
human's authorisation above. **Converged: `status: accepted`, both phases
`reviewed = 2026-08-10`.** Phase 1 is cleared to implement; Phase 2 follows it.

Round 3's blocker — the measured diamond bound zeroing the badge on the no-arms
path — was verified fixed **in the file, with the arithmetic redone**, not taken
on the changelog's word: `padRadius([], center, 1)` falls to `armWidth`'s
`MIN_ROAD_WIDTH` = 12, giving `rp` = 10.44 and a half-diagonal of **8.874** before
and after, so "byte-identical" is a real claim with a pinnable `points` string
rather than an aspiration. The gates reviewer additionally established the branch
is **exhaustive**: `arms.length > 0` ⟺ `padShape` non-empty, because `Arm.width`
is always positive (`laneWidths` floors an empty `lanes` array to one default
lane) and `armReach` floors `r ≥ |origin − center| + width/2`, so every band ∩
disc is non-empty and nothing can land between the two states.

**The boundary convention turned out to be load-bearing, not merely a trap** —
the round's most useful finding, and it arrived as a confirmation rather than an
objection. Because the glyph centre lies exactly *on* every band's inner cut, a
boundary-inclusive `rayPadExit` is what makes all three of §2.3's worked examples
come out right: on §1's T the north ray rides the two bands' inner cuts and exits
at **19.5**, exactly the band edge §2.3 names as the target; on the
1-lane-through/4-lane-stem T it exits at **6**; on a four-arm cross every
direction reaches the disc, so `s = r` and the diamond is unchanged at `0.85 r`.
One convention, three correct answers — and a strict outside-test copied from
`rayCircleExit` would have collapsed every diamond to nothing.

Rotation numbers re-derived and confirmed: a T rotated 20° puts its north tip at
`23.1 · cos 20°` = **21.71** against a half-width of 19.5 (empty space), and at
45° at `23.1 · cos 45°` = **16.33** (on asphalt) — so "some rotations, not all"
is the correct statement.

One non-blocking finding, and **both reviewers raised it independently**: §2.3's
"`s` is `0` in exactly two situations" contradicted OQ-5 eight lines later, which
correctly adds a single-arm junction and a same-direction Y — neither of which
involves a median. The correctness reviewer reasoned explicitly about why this was
non-blocking where its round-3 ancestor was blocking: the normative rule an
implementer follows is `min(rp * 0.85, s)` plus the no-arms carve-out, which is
unaffected, so a vanishing badge is now a *recorded deferral* rather than a
contradiction with a stated invariant. Fixed before either verdict landed: §2.3
now points at OQ-5, and OQ-5 carries the full enumeration.

The gates reviewer also confirmed the phase's size and self-containment were not
disturbed by the carve-out, which reuses the existing no-arms branch rather than
adding one.

### Round 3 — document-wide — 2026-08-10 — both round-1 agents, resumed — **ESCALATED** (1 blocking)

**Split verdict**, and the cap reached: the gates/scope reviewer returned
`VERDICT: READY` with zero blocking; the correctness reviewer returned
`VERDICT: NOT READY` with one. Per §7.5 the loop **stops at three rounds** and
escalates rather than running a fourth, so **nothing is dated and `status` stays
`draft`** (§7.7). The outstanding blocker's fix is written into the spec but is
**unverified by a review round** — that is the state the human is deciding about.

**The blocker: the measured diamond bound zeroes the badge on the no-arms path.**
`rayPadExit` returns `0` when the point is outside every ring, and with no arms
`padShape` returns `[]` — so `s = 0` vacuously and the half-diagonal
`min(rp * 0.85, s)` is `0`, while that same branch still paints a full `<circle>`
of asphalt under the vanished diamond. Reachable by ordinary use: make a node a
junction, pick Priority, *then* draw the roads; or delete the links from a
priority junction. The fix applied — the disc fallback keeps today's
`rp * 0.85`, the measured bound governs only the derived pad, and the no-arms
gate assertion now covers the diamond as well as the pad. Two supporting
sentences were wrong for the same reason and were corrected: §2.3's enumeration
of when `s = 0`, and OQ-5's "needs every approach divided to reach".

Non-blocking, all accepted: §2.6's bullet still carried the signal-head claim
§2.3 had just retracted (**both** reviewers caught this — the bullet has now been
wrong twice and says so); "a rotated T puts all four tips in empty space" is
false at 45° (16.33 against 19.5) and true at 20° (21.7), so it became "some
rotations"; `rayPadExit` needed its boundary convention stated, because the glyph
centre lies exactly **on** every band's inner cut, so an implementation copying
`rayCircleExit`'s strict outside-test returns `0` for every ring and collapses
every diamond; OQ-5's reachability argument weakened (a **single-arm** junction
also gives `s = 0`); and OQ-1's inventory now separates the four `tsc`-caught
construction sites from the one `cargo`-caught read.

**Numbers this round re-measured**, so a later round can trust them: the divided
2-lane `dividedApproach` fixture gives `rayCircleExit` = √(24² − 13.5²) = 19.84
against a furthest vertex at √(24² − 3²) = 23.81; the divided 4-lane case gives
35.46 against 41.89; the 10°/chord rule gives a sagitta of 0.1034 at `r` = 27.18
and **0.471 at the largest pad the app can produce** (8 lanes at the Inspector's
2.5× Size clamp, `r` = 123.75) — under half a unit, with little margin; and the
1-lane-through/4-lane-stem T measures `s` = 6 against a width-derived 19.5.

The gates/scope reviewer **withdrew its own round-2 question** on splitting Phase
1: the diamond bound is the only real seam, and splitting there would ship an
intermediate phase painting yellow on bare paper.

### Round 2 — document-wide — 2026-08-10 — both round-1 agents, resumed — **NOT READY** (4 blocking after dedupe)

Both reviewers returned NOT READY, and **converged independently on two defects**,
which is the panel earning its keep.

- **`padInradius`'s contract is false — and the function was invented in round 1
  to fix a round-1 finding.** This is §3's "a fix can introduce a blocker" in its
  purest form. §2.2 cuts every band at a half-plane through the centre, so one arm
  contributes only a **half**-disc and a `max` over arms is not an inradius. Three
  counterexamples between the two reviewers: a single-arm junction, a Y whose arms
  all leave the same way, and — needing no unusual layout at all — a T with a
  1-lane through road and a 4-lane stem, which still floats 13.5 units of yellow.
  **Resolved by deletion**: the bound is now measured with `rayPadExit`, and the
  width-derived form is recorded as tried-and-wrong so it is not re-derived.
- **The replacement reach assertion measured the wrong quantity**, found from two
  different fixtures (35.46 vs 41.89; 19.84 vs 23.81). It stated a claim about a
  **ray** as a claim about the ring's furthest **vertex**; those differ whenever a
  carriageway is displaced, so a *correct* implementation would have failed the
  gate by 4–6 units. Re-phrased on the ray and checked with an independent
  point-in-any-ring predicate — deliberately **not** through `rayPadExit`, or a
  bug in the helper could mask a bug in the rings it measures.
- **"Opposite the missing arm" names a direction where an arm exists.** On a T
  with arms E/W/S it reads as due *south*, which is the stem; the arithmetic in
  the same sentence only works for due *north*. Both this and the earlier "empty
  quadrant" wording are now recorded as failed, so neither is re-tried.
- **The chord density of the outer arc was unspecified**, and containment passes
  trivially for any inscribed arc, so nothing could catch a coarse one. Now
  bounded at 10° per chord **and** with a vertex forced at the arm's own ray exit,
  which turns the reach assertion from an approximation into an equality.

One decision was **refuted and its conclusion kept**: §2.3 claimed `SignalHead`
sits outside the pad "by construction". Its *anchor* is `rp + 7 * scale`, but the
body is a rect whose inner corner reaches 15.18 against `rp` = 16.02. The head is
still untouched — because roadside furniture needs no asphalt beneath it, which
is a different reason and now the stated one.

### Round 1 — document-wide — 2026-08-10 — panel of two clean-room agents with repo access — **NOT READY** (9 blocking after dedupe)

A panel rather than one generalist, on §7.1's "large or high-blast-radius"
clause: Phase 1 changes how **every** junction in every document draws, and Phase
2 carries the project's first migration. Lenses: correctness/grounding, and
exit-gate testability/scope/cross-file consistency.

**Round 0** (this episode = the whole document, no phase having shipped): does
this produce the observable, and is it the right one? **Yes.** Phase 1's output
is the drawn junction in every figure. The challenge recorded against it, and not
re-litigated afterwards: a metro map draws a station as a dot, so is a disc a
legitimate *schematic* abstraction rather than a defect? **No** — this project
draws roads with real widths, lane bands and edge lines, so a junction that
ignores its arms is inconsistent with everything drawn around it. Phase 2
produces no observable and argues it explicitly, as `CLAUDE.md` requires.

Every finding was **accepted; none rejected**. The three that reshaped the spec:

- **The central claim was true for the wrong reason.** §2.3 said the rim
  consumers survive "because an arm's band runs the full radius" — ambiguous, and
  on a divided approach the two readings differ by 4–6 units. Worse, a flat-ended
  band puts its corners at √(r² + (w/2)²), *outside* the hit disc and halo (33.45
  against 29.18 on §1's T). The pad must be **clipped to the disc**, and that
  containment, not the union, is what keeps the three consumers correct.
- **The gate was vacuous.** All three rim consumers read `padRadius` and none
  reads the outline, so the stop bars are byte-identical *whatever* `padShape`
  returns — including a wrong one. What the spec called its proof was a
  regression check, and it now says so.
- **A regression the spec had explicitly waved off.** §2.6's "the badges are
  untouched" was false: `diamondPoints(rp * 0.85)` is inside a disc and outside a
  union of bands, floating 3.6 units of yellow on §1's own T.

Also: the headline gate assertion was **false as written** — a point at 45° into
the "empty quadrant" is *inside* the through road's band, the genuinely empty arc
being only ±37°; six shipped `class="jn-pad" r="…"` assertions (one byte-exact)
would have been broken silently and are now named with their replacement;
`padShape` could not return "today's disc" as its no-arms fallback, given its own
return type; and OQ-3's premise was geometrically unfounded — two arms 5° apart
cannot leave a sliver, and the real gap is a divided road's **median**, which is
correct and stays open. OQ-3 was **resolved from the geometry** rather than
deferred, per §4's rule about code-answerable questions.

Both reviewers verified §1's arithmetic independently (4 lanes → 39 wide, `rp`
27.18, edge 19.5, bulge 7.68; 1 lane → 12/10.44/6/4.44) and both confirmed the
two load-bearing historical claims: `git log -S` shows `t_junction` in the
**initial commit**, predating the spec corpus, so no phase ever shipped it and
`supersedes: null` is right; and `JunctionView.rotation` is read by nothing that
draws. §2.1 was rewritten to work §6.1's test **step by step** after a reviewer
noted the draft argued step 1 and skipped step 2, whose competing answer —
append a phase to `zk-005`, which owns junction interiors and is `done` — is now
stated and rejected on the record.
